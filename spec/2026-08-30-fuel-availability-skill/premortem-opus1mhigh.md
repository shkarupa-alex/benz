A pre-mortem is only useful if it attacks the design's own assumptions rather than generic project risks, so it's worth naming what this design actually bets on before naming what breaks. It makes four load-bearing bets: (a) that `agent-browser` can sustain unattended access to three sites every 15 minutes for months; (b) that Yandex's unversioned internal `fuelAvailability` representation stays parseable, and that when it doesn't, the failure will be *visible*; (c) that the three sources are meaningfully redundant, so failure isolation yields a still-useful result; and (d) that a heartbeat automation attached to "the current Codex task" gives a stable execution environment, persistent local state, and a reliable stop signal. The empirical observations quoted in the task already put pressure on (a) and (c) on day one — 2GIS was CAPTCHA'd, gdebenz was 502, and only Yandex carried per-grade status, timestamps, and queue data. The scenarios below follow those bets to their failure points.

---

## Scenario 1 — The monitor becomes a well-behaved null generator

**What happened.** Monitoring ran for weeks, then Yandex began serving challenges (or empty result sets) to the automated session. The skill did exactly what it was designed to do: it emitted `CHALLENGE`, refused to bypass, kept the cadence, and made degradation prominent. Six months later the task contains thousands of correctly formatted reports that say "источники: Яндекс — CHALLENGE, 2ГИС — CHALLENGE, gdebenz — HTTP_ERROR; станций со свежими данными: 0." The user stopped reading them after the second day and checked fuel by driving.

**Why it happened.** The design has a strong stance for a *single tick* — degraded is better than wrong — but no stance across ticks. "Monitoring does not auto-stop merely because data is degraded. It continues at the requested cadence" is a rule with no escape hatch and no notion of a *persistently* useless monitor. Combined with an all-`agent-browser` transport, a fixed 15-minute cadence, and persistent named sessions per source, the design also maximizes the bot-detection signal it generates against the one source it depends on: the same profile, same origin, same interval, indefinitely. Nothing in the design treats access budget as a scarce resource to be spent carefully.

**What would have prevented it.** Distinguish *transient* degradation from *structural* degradation, with a rule expressed in ticks, not in a single run: after N consecutive ticks in which no source produced any fresh, grade-specific observation, escalate once loudly, then back off the cadence (or self-pause with an explicit "resume monitoring?" message) instead of continuing to publish nulls. Pair that with cadence jitter and a per-source access budget so a 15-minute nominal interval doesn't mean a metronome-perfect fingerprint, and with a deliberate decision that monitoring's *quality of last resort* is "last known good, aged X hours, explicitly labelled" rather than an empty report.

## Scenario 2 — Schema drift arrives as `UNKNOWN`, not as `SCHEMA_CHANGED`

**What happened.** Yandex changed how the results page hydrates: station cards still render titles, addresses, and coordinates, but the per-grade availability block is now populated lazily and is absent from the state the adapter evaluates. The adapter kept returning a full, plausible list of stations for the configured area — with every grade at `UNKNOWN`, no `lastSignalTimestamp`, and no queue. Source health said `OK`. The report said, correctly per its own rules, "нет свежих данных" for every station. Nobody noticed for weeks that this was a parser regression rather than a quiet week on the crowd-report side.

**Why it happened.** The data model treats `UNKNOWN` as a legitimate *source-provided* availability status, and `ObservationTime` has an `UNKNOWN` kind — both are normal, expected values. The extraction layer has no way to distinguish "the source told us it doesn't know" from "the field we expected wasn't there." The design's guardrail — "never convert parser failure into an empty result" — protects against the *empty* failure mode but not against the *degenerate-but-populated* one, which is the more likely shape when a page partially changes. `SCHEMA_CHANGED` is listed as a health code but the design specifies no trigger condition for emitting it beyond "extraction failed."

**What would have prevented it.** Make structural absence a distinct outcome from semantic unknown, at the adapter boundary: a per-tick extraction invariant asserting expected field presence (e.g., if the station list is non-empty but the fraction of stations carrying any fuel block or any timestamp falls below a configured floor, the result is `SCHEMA_CHANGED`, not a list of `UNKNOWN`s), plus a recorded schema fingerprint compared each tick. Also make "all stations unknown" an explicitly alarming report state rather than a routine one.

## Scenario 3 — Failure isolation was satisfied; usefulness was not

**What happened.** The skill met its stated requirement — a failing source never prevented other sources from producing a result — and yet every time Yandex was unavailable, the report contained nothing actionable for the default query. 2GIS contributed station names and "normally offers AI-95," which the design assigns zero current-availability weight. gdebenz, when reachable, is grade-blind for branded/premium variants, and the design's own rule says a source that cannot represent premium AI-95 cannot confirm or deny it. So the default query — base AI-95 plus every configured premium variant — was answerable by exactly one source, and the multi-source architecture never paid off.

**Why it happened.** The design reasoned about failure isolation at the level of *process* (one adapter's error doesn't cancel another's) rather than at the level of *capability* (does the surviving set of sources answer the question the user actually asked?). Meanwhile a substantial amount of machinery was built on the assumption of redundancy: cross-source station identity matching with one-to-one assignment and manual overrides, a persistent station registry, conflict windows, `ПРОТИВОРЕЧИВО` verdicts, and independence rules for confidence boosting. With one contributing source, none of those paths meaningfully execute, but all of them must be maintained and all of them are places for bugs to hide — for example the identity matcher producing duplicate rows for the same station across pagination batches, a path that gets little real exercise.

**What would have prevented it.** Write an explicit per-source capability matrix — which sources can produce grade-specific, timestamped, current observations for which products — and check it against the default query *before* committing to the architecture. Had that been done, the visible conclusion would be "this is a single-source system with two enrichment sources," which points investment somewhere else entirely: resilience and repairability for the one real source (including reconsidering the deferred official 2GIS API, or a second independent access shape for Yandex), a last-known-good cache with explicit aging, and a much smaller reconciliation layer that can grow if and when a second availability source actually materializes.

## Scenario 4 — The heartbeat outlived the environment its state depended on

**What happened.** Two problems appeared together. First, "changes since the previous tick" was almost always missing or wrong: some replays found no previous snapshot at all and re-reported everything as new, others compared against a snapshot hours old. Second, when the user wrote "stop" in the conversation, the automation sometimes kept firing, and disabling it required going after the automation directly rather than through the skill.

**Why it happened.** The design deliberately moved mutable state (snapshots, station registry, identity overrides, browser health history) outside the skill directory, and deliberately removed all scheduling and lifecycle logic from the scripts, delegating both to a native heartbeat attached to the current task. That is sound reasoning about *skill upgrades*, but it silently assumes each heartbeat replay lands in the same filesystem and the same browser-profile context as the previous one. The design never states that contract. Everything valuable at monitoring time — diffs, the identity registry, warm authenticated browser sessions — depends on it. Similarly, "until the user asks to stop" is a natural-language condition evaluated by whichever invocation happens to see it, while the thing that must actually be torn down is an automation object; the design describes stopping as a capability but defines no idempotent teardown, no confirmation that teardown succeeded, and no self-expiry if the conversation is abandoned.

**What would have prevented it.** State the execution-environment contract explicitly and design for its weakest form: assume a replay may be cold, keyed by a stable state path that's verified at startup, with the report saying "базовый снимок недоступен — сравнение не выполнено" rather than silently producing a bogus diff. Make stop an explicit, idempotent operation whose success is confirmed back to the user, and give every monitoring session a TTL / maximum tick count so an abandoned task cannot leave an automation running indefinitely.

## Scenario 5 — The thresholds were never tuned, because the design never generated the evidence to tune them

**What happened.** The default freshness bands and the confidence caps stayed exactly as shipped. In real shortage conditions — precisely when the skill was supposed to earn its keep — signals were sparse and station state flipped faster than the 45-minute "fresh" band, so most rows landed on `НЕТ СВЕЖИХ ДАННЫХ` or `КОСВЕННО / низкая`, and the ranked top-5 was frequently empty. The user's recurring question, "is 45 minutes right, or should it be 20?", was never answerable, and the open question about tuning thresholds "after a short period of real observations" was still open six months later.

**Why it happened.** The design rejected pseudo-probability specifically because "no ground-truth calibration supports a percentage" — a defensible call — but then built no mechanism that would ever produce calibration data. Snapshots are written per tick and used for diffing, but nothing accumulates the quantities you'd need to tune: how long a station stays `IN_STOCK`, how often a fresh positive is followed within one tick by a negative, the distribution of verdicts and confidence levels over time, or any signal of whether a `ЕСТЬ` claim matched reality on arrival. The verdict/confidence table is exhaustively unit-tested for internal consistency, which proves the logic matches its own specification but says nothing about whether the specification tracks the world. The result is a system that is unfalsifiable by construction and therefore permanently untunable.

**What would have prevented it.** Treat the thresholds as parameters under measurement from the first run: persist a compact per-tick observation log (verdict and confidence distribution, observed signal ages, station state-transition intervals) alongside the snapshots, and add one lightweight ground-truth capture path — a way for the user to record "заехал, было / не было" against a specific station and timestamp. Even a handful of such records per week turns the open question into an answerable one, and would let the categorical scale be validated (or collapsed to fewer, more honest levels) instead of frozen at its initial guess.

## One smaller trap worth noting

The `station-anchors` area mode combines `bufferMeters: 0`, `unresolvedPolicy: "fail-closed"`, and anchor resolution *through the browser adapters*. Two consequences follow directly: area configuration becomes impossible during exactly the challenge conditions that motivate careful design, and the named outermost stations sit precisely on the convex hull, so any coordinate revision or cross-source coordinate difference of a few tens of meters silently drops the very stations the user declared acceptable. A non-zero default buffer, or pinning anchors as always-included by station key independent of geometry, removes the trap at no cost to the "don't silently expand the area" intent.
